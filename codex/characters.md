# Characters

Characters of the Zone and of the occupation, in the order they were filed. Marks follow the taxonomy: CHROMA\_VAL for the ten primary characters, TORUS\_VAL for secondary characters, GRID\_FAIL for the occupiers. Ages are given as the manuscript gives them. Where two passages give different ages, both are recorded, because the testimonies were taken at different times.

| Field | Entry |

| Name | Diana Antonoff |
| Category | Characters. PROB\_VAL\_0.9 / CHROMA\_VAL |
| Summary | Ukrainian journalist, aged 28, who documented the survivor community and assembled the Protocols. Disappeared on the night of the third flood. |
| Entry | Diana Antonoff was a Ukrainian journalist who filed dispatches to Suspilne. She lived alone on an abandoned houseboat moored at Żerań, at the northern edge of the wetlands, where the others said the water ran quietest. She disappeared from the survivor outpost at Czerniaków on the night of the third flood, in the first January of the third year of the occupation. She was twenty-eight. Her last dispatch was filed the Tuesday before. No body was recovered and no statement was issued. The community she had documented entered her name among the missing and continued the work she had taught them. She is the source of the Codex marks and of the recorded testimonies. A nine-minute tape of her voice survives and is used once in the archive. |

## SYNPTCL/CHR/002 Goya Liszka

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/CHR/002 |
| Name | Goya Liszka |
| Category | Characters. PROB\_VAL\_0.9 / CHROMA\_VAL |
| Summary | Veteran of Mariupol and the Runners' guide through the wetlands. Swears without pause. Hears drones before anyone else. |
| Entry | Goya Liszka served in the last cellar at Mariupol, under what had been a bakery. Ten men held it. Nine died when a shell came through. He was at the back of the room and lay three days among the dead because to move was to be heard. No camp or column would take him afterwards, because he does not sleep and watches the door. The Zone took him because it asked only what he could do. He can hear a drone in the air before anyone lifts a head, and he reads a man's route from the mud on his boots. He knows where every Runner in the wetlands is at any hour, and which chimneys at Żerań still carry a watcher. He swears at everything: the boat, the sky, the dead, God, his own hands. The archivist records him as the most damaged person he met in the Zone and the most necessary. He speaks to children and drones in the same voice. In the clinic ward he called Pawel "the banker" and told him to count a Runner's blood on the floor, and was told by Catalina that he was bleeding on her floor, which he was. He stood at the door when Lucasz was locked in the control room. |
| Appears in | His own Protocol (Mariupol testimony); Protocol 001 (the table column headed Watch); Lucasz's Protocol; Catalina's clinic scene |
| Reliability | C (3). Mariupol account is his own and is consistent across the passages read. His age of 42 is in the project brief only (U). |

| Entry | Fritha Cielinski was nineteen when the archivist met her. A Runner at the door, who would not come in, gave her name and said only that the girl in the church was not to be hurried. She recalls being told she was nineteen and thinking it the most unfair thing anyone had said to her. |

| Summary | Medic who runs the clinic. Aged 40 at first account, 41 later. |
| Entry | Before the war Catalina Chmielewska was a software engineer in Warsaw, at a firm that made scheduling systems for hospitals. After the invasion she trained as a volunteer medic.
