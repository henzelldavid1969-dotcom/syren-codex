## SYNPTCL/OBJ/001 Dendron

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/OBJ/001 |
| Name | Dendron |
| Category | FLUX_LOCK_09 / MAG_REF_400 |
| Summary | An organism of great age, recovered from permafrost, that spreads with floodwater and sedates those who stay near it. |
| Entry | The Dendron is the organism that spread through the Warsaw corridor with the flood. The name is a survivor's name. Dendron is Greek for tree, and the organism is not a tree. It is reported to be of great age, recovered from permafrost in northern Siberia before the war, studied in Soviet laboratories for decades and made available for military use in the last years before the invasion. A Russian scientist who fled describes it as the first thing that was alive before anyone decided what alive meant. It climbs walls, pipes, railings and the iron of tram wires, and prefers damp, shade and the dark inside things. It clogs drains and sewers, so that the water rises and the organism grows in the new water. It makes a sustained tone at approximately 180 Hz with a slow beat of about six seconds, loudest at night and over still air. Those exposed to the compound do not appear to hear it. It emits a compound that is not a toxin and does not kill. It sedates. The effect is recorded in four stages, from unusual calm at stage 1 to obedience to any instruction at stage 4. In winter the visible growth retreats. Below 8 degrees Celsius the organism closes, and exposure is much less dangerous. Exposure is reduced by distance, by cold, by keeping upwind, and by filtration. It does not grow on the bricks of the Żerań power station, and no reason is known. The stem is the width of a finger and the colour of wet slate, and it has no leaves. Paired structures along it, about nine centimetres apart, show grey-white when open above 8 degrees Celsius. At the Żerań cooling channel a mature mass lowered the water level by 11 centimetres in nine days. In summer it is dense and hides. Wind is the main way exposure travels in the open, and the Filtry water station supplies the only water in the corridor reliably free of the compound. Catalina Chmielewska had treated eighty-one persons with documented exposure when the Protocol was compiled, and three of them did not become compliant. At the Zoo an old woman, Mrs Kaczmarek, leaves milk at the foot of the largest mass each morning at seven and has stayed at stage 1 for three months. The Russian word for what it does, mir, is also the word for peace. A hum with a six second beat is heard in Interlude IV and Interlude VI, and a sound of 180 hertz with the same beat is played beneath the interview table in Interlude III. The manuscript does not say that these are the Dendron's tone. |

## SYNPTCL/OBJ/002 Dendron Dust

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/OBJ/002 |
| Name | Dendron Dust |
| Category | FLUX_LOCK_09 / SHIFT_REF_04 |
| Summary | A pale powder scraped from the surface of mature Dendron growth. |
| Entry | The surface of a mature mass of the Dendron carries a pale powder in dry conditions. It can be scraped off. It is known in the corridor as Dendron Dust. In the dry months of summer the wind lifts it from the surface and carries it some distance, and it is then more widely spread than in any other season. Wet, it does not keep. Where it falls into standing water it dissolves, and the water that takes it is afterwards, in its effect, no different from water that carries the compound. Raw, it carries a trace of the compound, and breathed in on the wind it dulls as the water does. Prepared by those who gather it, scraped, dried and cut, and taken in the amounts used, it has the opposite effect. It is gathered in the cold months, when the organism is slow, and not in summer. Taken, the dust acts as a psychedelic. Members of the resistance use it in rituals. |
| Appears in | Chapter 9: The Dendron (Protocol 003), section 7, Dendron Dust. Chapter 12: The Codex (Protocol 004), section 4.3, where the secondary mark for the Dust is given. |
| Reliability | C (1 substantive passage, Protocol 003 section 7, with a second mention in Protocol 004 section 4.3). The spread by wind and the dissolving in water are graded C by the Protocol, two sources, not confirmed clinically. The psychedelic use and the rituals rest on that passage alone, reported to Diana and not attended by her: E. |

## SYNPTCL/OBJ/004 Appendix: The Brief

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/OBJ/004 |
| Name | Appendix: The Brief |
| Category | Objects. FLUX_LOCK_09 / FLUX_SYNC |
| Summary | The document that names the programme behind the Dendron. |
| Entry | The Brief is the document that Diana received on 28 December 2034, which Protocol 005 describes in part and which Alex reproduces in full in the appendix of the manuscript, from the second binder. It records Operation Neural Harvest, its origins, the parties that supplied the organism and the science, and its stated aim, population compliance optimisation, set down without comment. It is eleven pages, in Russian and in English, on the stationery of a research directorate, stamped, numbered and signed with initials. Diana does not copy the names of the Western partners, on the ground that a binder can be found. In the appendix each place where a partner name stood is marked withheld, and the initials of the signatories are not copied either. The brief is marked Restricted and copy 3 of 5, gives ethical review as not applicable, and is distributed to the Directorate, the Operator and three partner establishments. The brief describes the survivors, about forty thousand, as a test cohort, the flood as a delivery system, the stages of the organism's effect as behavioural endpoints, and stage 4 as the target state. It gives the city's qualities as isolated, contained and non-attributable. It holds the results in a joint repository, to be shared with partners on completion of the cohort phase, sets a target of not less than 60 per cent of the cohort at stage 3 or above within 90 days of continuous exposure, and expects a small proportion of the population to show atypical resistance. Four annexes, A to D, are listed in it and are not reproduced. Diana reads it four times on 28 December 2034 and finds the tidiness of it the worst part. In Interlude V a brief of matching wording is on slide four of a deck. The manuscript does not say that it is the same document. |

| Summary | Forty-one family photographs recovered from the flood. |
| Entry | Forty-one prints were carried in her father's jacket, which Fritha found on a railing six days after the flood. They were dried on a radiator in a stairwell without power. The emulsion had lifted and her mother's face had shifted a little to the left. Her family had gone back for eleven albums in the forty minutes after the dam went, and the prints are what she took out of the water. She dried them in front of a stove made from a bucket. Her father was a photographer with a studio in Praga, and her mother worked in conservation at the National Museum. The forty-one began a larger collection. By 9 August 2034 Diana found several thousand photographs in rows on the altar steps of St Michael's in Praga, each weighted at the corners with a pebble or a spent cartridge and sorted by year. |
