## Dendron

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/OBJ/001 |
| Name | Dendron |
| Category | FLUX\_LOCK\_09 / MAG\_REF\_400 |
| Summary | An organism of great age, recovered from permafrost, that spreads with floodwater and sedates those who stay near it. |
| Entry | The Dendron is the organism that spread through the Warsaw corridor with the flood. The name is a survivor's name. Dendron is Greek for tree, and the organism is not a tree. It is reported to be of great age, recovered from permafrost in northern Siberia before the war, studied in Soviet laboratories for decades and made available for military use in the last years before the invasion. A Russian scientist who fled describes it as the first thing that was alive before anyone decided what alive meant. It climbs walls, pipes, railings and the iron of tram wires, and prefers damp, shade and the dark inside things. It clogs drains and sewers, so that the water rises and the organism grows in the new water. It makes a sustained tone at approximately 180 Hz with a slow beat of about six seconds, loudest at night and over still air. Those exposed to the compound do not appear to hear it. It emits a compound that is not a toxin and does not kill. It sedates. The effect is recorded in four stages, from unusual calm at stage 1 to obedience to any instruction at stage 4. In winter the visible growth retreats. Below 8 degrees Celsius the organism closes, and exposure is much less dangerous. Exposure is reduced by distance, by cold, by keeping upwind, and by filtration. It does not grow on the bricks of the Żerań power station, and no reason is known. |
| Appears in | The opening pages, where the Protocols are first described. Protocol 003 (Chapter 9), in full. Protocol 001, section 9, for the seasonal variation. Protocol 005 and Appendix: The Brief. The Interludes in which the stages are used. |
| Reliability | The least reliable of the Protocols, by its own account: the organism has not been clinically studied. Spread: C, several sources. Sound: C, recorded on eleven nights by the archivist. Distance beyond about 40 metres: E. Vinegar mask, reduction by perhaps half: E, one source. Origin in Siberian permafrost: E, one source, the scientist. |
| Cross-references | SYNPTCL/OBJ/002 Dendron Dust. SYNPTCL/LOR/001 Operation Neural Harvest. |
| Media | The Dendron tone, 180 Hz with a six second beat. To be produced for the site and the podcast. |
| Status | Draft |

## Dendron Dust

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/OBJ/002 |
| Name | Dendron Dust |
| Category | FLUX\_LOCK\_09 / SHIFT\_REF\_04 |
| Summary | A pale powder scraped from the surface of mature Dendron growth. |
| Entry | The surface of a mature mass of the Dendron carries a pale powder in dry conditions. It can be scraped off. It is known in the corridor as Dendron Dust. In the dry months of summer the wind lifts it from the surface and carries it some distance, and it is then more widely spread than in any other season. Wet, it does not keep. Where it falls into standing water it dissolves, and the water that takes it is afterwards, in its effect, no different from water that carries the compound. Taken, the dust acts as a psychedelic. Members of the resistance use it in rituals. |
| Appears in | Protocol 003, section 7. Indirectly in Appendix: The Brief, section 3, which describes the surface material carrying the compound in the air in the dry months. |
| Reliability | C, two sources. Not confirmed clinically. |
| Cross-references | SYNPTCL/OBJ/001 Dendron. |
| Status | Draft. The psychedelic use and the rituals are canon by David's decision of 7 November 2026, and come from the older project note and the taxonomy. The manuscript does not yet say so. One line in Protocol 003, section 7, would bring the two into agreement. |
