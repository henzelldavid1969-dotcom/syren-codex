## SYNPTCL/LOR/001 Operation Neural Harvest

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/001 |
| Name | Operation Neural Harvest |
| Category | ION_PATH / LOAD_STRESS |
| Summary | The Russian research programme under which the surviving population of Warsaw was held as a test cohort. |
| Entry | Neural Harvest is the name of a research programme described in an operational brief that Diana Antonoff received on 28 December, through the Russian scientist she calls a source in Protocol 003. The brief is eleven pages, in Russian and in English, on the stationery of a research directorate, stamped and numbered. Hers was copy 3 of 5. The brief treats the survivors of the Warsaw corridor, estimated at forty thousand, as a test cohort. It treats the flood as the delivery system. It records the effects of the organism as behavioural endpoints in four stages and names stage 4 as the target state. The brief names loss of containment as the principal risk to the programme, and loss of the measurement through the emergence of an organised population as the secondary risk. It considers both low. A small proportion of the cohort is expected to show atypical resistance. Such subjects are to be identified, monitored and recorded. They are not to be removed from the cohort unless they affect the integrity of the measurement. The target for the cohort phase is that not less than 60 per cent of the cohort is assessed at stage 3 or above within 90 days of the establishment of continuous exposure. The phase ends when the Directorate determines that the measurement is complete. Results are held in a joint repository and are to be shared with the partner establishments on completion of the cohort phase. The brief gives ethical review as not applicable and states no reason. The name comes from the observation section: a harvest is the gathering of what has grown. The brief is reproduced as Appendix: The Brief, with the names of the Western partners and the initials withheld. It lists four annexes: A, the organism; B, delivery; C, behavioural endpoints; and D, observation, cordon and data. They are in the binder and are not reproduced. The programme is described as joint, run by the Russian state and at least two Western states through their agencies, military research establishments and private biotechnology contractors. The Russians supplied the organism and the Western partners supplied the means of enhancing it, stabilising it in a water supply and optimising it. The brief gives the grounds for the choice of city as isolated, contained and non-attributable, with no external access and no prospect of relief. The scientist gave Diana the name on 30 October 2034, and she wrote it in the second binder, not in Protocol 003. She believes the cohort phase ended with the destruction of Żerań on 1 January 2035, and rates that belief 0.6. In Interlude V an account lead works on a contract called the Programme, whose name has two words, one suggesting the brain and one a verb for gathering a crop. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/002 The stages of compliance

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/002 |
| Name | The stages of compliance |
| Category | Lore. ION_PATH / LOAD_STRESS |
| Summary | The occupiers' scale for assessing how far a person has been pacified. |
| Entry | The occupiers assess residents on a numbered scale. Stage 3 is the point at which a person will give up a name when asked. Stage 4 is the point at which they will give up one without being asked. Stage 5 is reported to the operator at R0 as not his concern. The district dashboard shows a cohort status, a percentage of the district assessed at Stage 3 or above, which stood at 71.2 per cent on the day recorded. The rota sends crews to collect persons assessed at Stage 4 who are non-ambulant. At the Poniatowski Bridge checkpoint, arrivals are assessed on the bridge at Stage 2 or 3 and passed to a reception hall. The scale is not the same as the medical staging of pressure sores, which one carer also records as Stage 2 and then Stage 4. Two accounts of the stages exist. Catalina Chmielewska's clinical table in Protocol 003 gives four, by time of continuous exposure, from unusual calm within four hours at stage 1 to compliance after four days at stage 4, where a person follows any instruction without hostility. The occupiers read the same stages by behaviour. In Interlude III, Budanov records that at stage 1 a subject refuses the water, at stage 2 drinks it quickly with a tremor, at stage 3 drinks it steadily and holds the glass in both hands, and at stage 4 neither drinks nor declines. A stage 0 appears in the screening records and on the dashboard in Interlude V, where a person at stage 0 is noted as resistant. The brief described in Protocol 005 names stage 4 as the target state. Protocol 005 also uses the word stage for the three phases of the destruction of Żerań, which is unrelated. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/003 The Warsaw Uprising of 1944

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/003 |
| Name | The Warsaw Uprising of 1944 |
| Category | Lore. ION_PATH / DIFF_REF_89 |
| Summary | The historical parallel the survivors and Diana draw on. |
| Entry | Marek Olszewski gave the following account. In 1944 the Home Army rose against the German occupation and fought for sixty-three days. It was destroyed. During those days the Red Army stood on the east bank of the Vistula, within sight of the city, and did not cross. When the rising failed, the German command razed what remained of the city, block by block, with explosives and fire, as punishment. Of the city on the left bank, eighty-five per cent was destroyed. The Old Town and Royal Castle were rebuilt afterwards from photographs and paintings. Marek also told the story of a Home Army captain in the sewers under Śródmieście in August: a hard man who held his line for three weeks because his men believed he would never leave them. In September the men found a note in his coat offering him terms. He had not accepted it, and had not burned it either. He kept it four days. Marek's judgement was that the men could not forgive that he was a good man who had kept the door open. Diana describes the destruction of Żerań in terms of 1944, and says the comparison was given to her and she declined to refuse it. Marek told Diana the account over tea, in a kitchen with the children under the table, and said he had spent ninety years waiting for someone to say it aloud. He told the story of the captain on Thursday 30 November 2034, to eleven people in his and Rosa's flat in Białołęka. Zoysa Zieliński was in the room and thanked him formally before leaving without her coat. Diana sets 1944 beside Żerań in a table of six rows and does not claim that the two are the same, only that the author of the second knew the first. Protocol 002 records the Old Town as occupied for its symbolism, with loudspeakers on the ramparts. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/005 Rising water

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/005 |
| Name | Rising water |
| Category | Lore. ION_PATH / DIFF_REF_89 |
| Summary | Why the flood does not recede. |
| Entry | The water has not receded because the causes have not been corrected. Reservoirs are damaged and cannot absorb rainfall. The dam at Włocławek is non-functional. Breached dikes cannot be repaired under occupation. Pumping stations have no power. The occupiers prioritise their own routes and show no interest in civilian drainage (E). The water plateaus and does not pass. Protocol 001 gives the causes as a cascade of five failures upstream between 11 and 16 October 2028, at Solina, Czorsztyn, Sandomierz, Włocławek and the Narew retention works. It puts the corridor at 84 m, plus or minus 0.4 m with rainfall, against a pre-flood datum of 77 m, read daily at the Żerań dock gauge. The explanation is graded E, and the manuscript shows the occupiers doing more than neglect the water. Lucasz Kowalski, who wrote the gate sequence of the Vistula Canal, says that those who took the control room swapped two lines, so that the canal brings water in. The third flood, on the night of 31 December 2034, is recorded as an act. The gates were opened in sequence with the lower gates held shut, and the Żerań gauge rose from 84.3 m at 21:00 to 88.6 m at 02:05. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/006 Screening

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/006 |
| Name | Screening |
| Category | Lore. ION_PATH / LOAD_STRESS |
| Summary | The occupiers' seven step procedure for establishing the stage of each subject, as run by Major Budanov at Aleja Szucha. |
| Entry | Screening is the procedure by which the occupiers establish the stage of each person brought to them. Protocol 002 names the screening camps and their head, Major Budanov, and says that what the screening consists of is not known to Diana. Interlude III gives the procedure. It is seven steps, on pages 61 to 88 of a ring-bound procedures binder of 212 pages, in Russian with a Polish translation. A committee wrote it. Budanov revised it in his first week, to take out what was unnecessary, and the word distress in its opening sentence is his. That sentence gives the purpose: to establish, in the shortest time and with the least distress, the stage of each subject. The steps are reception, observation, baseline, the offer, questions, recording and disposition. In observation the subject is watched in silence for between ninety seconds and three minutes, and the stage is read from what the subject does with a glass of water. At stage 1 the subject refuses it. At stage 2 the subject drinks it quickly, with a tremor. At stage 3 the subject drinks it steadily and holds the glass in both hands. At stage 4 the subject neither drinks nor declines. The baseline is five questions, on name, address, date of birth, occupation and nearest relations, with the delay before each answer noted in seconds. In the offer a boiled sweet is held out. About half of the subjects at stage 3 take it, and all at stage 4. The questions then ask for the names of persons hostile to the order of the district, without a raised voice or any touch. A clerk behind glass transcribes the answers in triplicate. The interviewer writes the stage and one of four decisions: Release, Retain, Transfer or Treat. The last two are not explained on any form that Alex has read. A one page annex to the binder, headed Acoustic Conditioning and unsigned, gives a low sound of about 180 hertz with a beat of six seconds, to be played beneath the table for a subject in the first days of exposure. It is not one of the seven steps, and the subject is not told of it. The screening record runs to 4,100 numbered forms, found in eleven boxes in a basement in Praga. Alex has read about three hundred. On one form the box for the sweet is blank. The subject is an interpreter, a woman of forty-one, assessed at stage 0, and the margin carries a note in Budanov's hand that she did not take it and that he is curious about her. The room, the lamp and the manner of the interviewer in the reconstruction are Alex's own and are not tagged to a source. |
