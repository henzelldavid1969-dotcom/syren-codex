## Operation Neural Harvest

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/001 |
| Name | Operation Neural Harvest |
| Category | ION\_PATH / LOAD\_STRESS |
| Summary | The Russian research programme under which the surviving population of Warsaw was held as a test cohort. |
| Entry | Neural Harvest is the name of a research programme described in an operational brief that Diana Antonoff received on 28 December, through the Russian scientist she calls a source in Protocol 003. The brief is eleven pages, in Russian and in English, on the stationery of a research directorate, stamped and numbered. Hers was copy 3 of 5. The brief treats the survivors of the Warsaw corridor, estimated at forty thousand, as a test cohort. It treats the flood as the delivery system. It records the effects of the organism as behavioural endpoints in four stages and names stage 4 as the target state. The programme does not seek to eliminate the population, since a population that has been eliminated cannot be observed. Less than one per cent of the cohort is expected to show atypical resistance, and those persons are to be recorded and not removed. The cohort phase is complete when not less than sixty per cent of blocks are reported at stage 3 or above for ninety consecutive days, and the reporting map is the instrument of completion. Results are to be shared with partners named in an annex. No ethical review is required, because the cohort is in a theatre of operations. The name comes from the observation section: a harvest is the gathering of what has grown. |
| Appears in | Protocol 005 (Chapter 15), the entry for 28 December. Appendix: The Brief, in full. The Interludes in which the cohort map and the stage counts are read. |
| Reliability | The brief as a document: seen and copied by the archivist, C. Its route to her: one intermediary, E. Annexes A to D were not attached to her copy and have not been seen. |
| Cross-references | SYNPTCL/OBJ/001 Dendron. SYNPTCL/OBJ/002 Dendron Dust. |
| Media | Facsimile of the brief, from Appendix: The Brief. To be designed. |
| Status | Draft. The older project entry gave only a rationale for the name, and it is replaced by this one. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/002 The stages of compliance

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/002 |
| Name | The stages of compliance |
| Category | Lore. ION\_PATH / LOAD\_STRESS |
| Summary | The occupiers' scale for assessing how far a person has been pacified. |
| Entry | The occupiers assess residents on a numbered scale. Stage 3 is the point at which a person will give up a name when asked. Stage 4 is the point at which they will give up one without being asked. Stage 5 is reported to the operator at R0 as not his concern. The district dashboard shows a cohort status, a percentage of the district assessed at Stage 3 or above, which stood at 71.2 per cent on the day recorded. The rota sends crews to collect persons assessed at Stage 4 who are non-ambulant. At the Poniatowski Bridge checkpoint, arrivals are assessed on the bridge at Stage 2 or 3 and passed to a reception hall. The scale is not the same as the medical staging of pressure sores, which one carer also records as Stage 2 and then Stage 4. |
| Appears in | Interlude II; Interlude with the checkpoint reports; Protocol 005 |
| Reliability | E (3). Stages 3 and 4 are defined in one source. Stages 1 and 2 are not defined in the passages read. |
| Cross-references | SYNPTCL/LOR/001; SYNPTCL/OBJ/001; SYNPTCL/CHR/020; SYNPTCL/CHR/022; SYNPTCL/CHR/023 |
| Status | Filed. Decide: Stages 1 and 2 are left undefined until the Protocol 005 text is audited. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/003 The Warsaw Uprising of 1944

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/003 |
| Name | The Warsaw Uprising of 1944 |
| Category | Lore. ION\_PATH / DIFF\_REF\_89 |
| Summary | The historical parallel the survivors and the archivist draw on. |
| Entry | Marek Olszewski gave the following account. In 1944 the Home Army rose against the German occupation and fought for sixty-three days. It was destroyed. During those days the Red Army stood on the east bank of the Vistula, within sight of the city, and did not cross. When the rising failed, the German command razed what remained of the city, block by block, with explosives and fire, as punishment. Of the city on the left bank, eighty-five per cent was destroyed. The Old Town and Royal Castle were rebuilt afterwards from photographs and paintings. Marek also told the story of a Home Army captain in the sewers under Śródmieście in August: a hard man who held his line for three weeks because his men believed he would never leave them. In September the men found a note in his coat offering him terms. He had not accepted it, and had not burned it either. He kept it four days. Marek's judgement was that the men could not forgive that he was a good man who had kept the door open. The archivist describes the destruction of Żerań in terms of 1944, and says the comparison was given to him and he declined to refuse it. |
| Appears in | Marek Olszewski's testimony; Protocol 002 section 5.3; the Żerań account |
| Reliability | C (2). Marek's testimony and the archivist's account. |
| Cross-references | SYNPTCL/CHR/015; SYNPTCL/LOR/004; SYNPTCL/LOC/017; SYNPTCL/LOC/028 |
| Status | Filed. Flag for the author: Marek is described as a child in August 1944, which would make him about 100 in 2034. Decide: left as the manuscript has it and recorded in the decisions list. |

## SYNPTCL/LOR/005 Rising water

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Reference | SYNPTCL/LOR/005 |
| Name | Rising water |
| Category | Lore. ION\_PATH / DIFF\_REF\_89 |
| Summary | Why the flood does not recede. |
| Entry | The water has not receded because the causes have not been corrected. Reservoirs are damaged and cannot absorb rainfall. The dam at Włocławek is non-functional. Breached dikes cannot be repaired under occupation. Pumping stations have no power. The occupiers prioritise their own routes and show no interest in civilian drainage (E). The water plateaus and does not pass. |

| Reliability | E (1). Stated as the archivist's assessment. |
