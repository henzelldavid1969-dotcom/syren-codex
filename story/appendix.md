**RESEARCH DIRECTORATE** **OPERATIONAL BRIEF** **Programme: NEURAL HARVEST** Eleven pages. Russian and English. Copy 3 of 5. Stamped and numbered.

## 1. Objective

The objective of the programme is to establish the conditions under which a civilian population, exposed to the organism over a sustained period, passes through the four recorded stages and remains at stage 4. The secondary objective is to record the behaviour of that population at each stage, in the field, at scale. The programme does not seek to eliminate the population. A population that has been eliminated cannot be observed.

## 2. Test cohort

The test cohort is the surviving civilian population of the Warsaw corridor, estimated at forty thousand persons. The cohort is held within a defined area. Movement into and out of the area is controlled. Movement within the area is permitted, since it is the movement that is the subject of study.

A small proportion of the cohort, estimated at less than one per cent, will display atypical resistance to the organism. These persons are to be recorded and not removed. Atypical resistance is a result.

## 3. Delivery

The flood is the delivery system. Floodwater carries the compound through the cohort area without the need for any act of delivery by personnel. In the dry months the surface material of the organism carries the compound in the air, and in the wet months it is carried by the water. Acoustic conditioning is applied in support and is described in Annex C. Annexes A to D are held by the directorate and are not attached to this copy.

## 4. Behavioural endpoints

The effects of the organism are recorded as behavioural endpoints, in four stages.

- Stage 1: elevated mood and unusual calm. Reversible.
- Stage 2: loss of urgency. Flattened affect. Tasks begun and not finished.
- Stage 3: apathy. Obedience to simple instructions. Loss of initiative. Speech normal.
- Stage 4: the target state. The subject follows any instruction from any source, without hostility, and without apparent memory of having refused.

Stage 4 is the target state of the programme.

## 5. Observation

The cohort is to be observed for the whole of the cohort phase. The following are to be recorded: decisions taken, speech, the rate of deterioration, the points at which the cohort resists and the points at which it does not. Observation is to be conducted by personnel who have themselves been conditioned to the same standard, so that the observers do not influence the result.

The programme takes its name from this section. A harvest is the gathering of what has grown.

## 6. Completion of the cohort phase

The cohort phase is complete when not less than sixty per cent of the blocks of the cohort area are reported at stage 3 or above for a period of ninety consecutive days. The reporting map is the instrument of completion.

## 7. Results and partners

The results of the programme are held in a joint repository. The results will be shared with partners on completion of the cohort phase. The partners are named in Annex D. \[Names not copied.\]

## 8. Review

No ethical review is required. The cohort is in a theatre of operations.
