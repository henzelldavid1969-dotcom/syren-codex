## Back cover

In 2028 the tanks crossed into Poland, and Warsaw fell within weeks. Then came the floods.

By 2034 the capital is a drowned, occupied city, and something is moving through the floodwater, older than the army that weaponised it and older than the city it is swallowing. It spreads like a vine, clogs the sewers, and breathes out a calm that feels almost like mercy. People stop resisting. People stop caring. Nobody can say exactly when it happens to them. In the occupiers' hands the mind itself becomes a battlefield, and memory is the weapon.

Diana Antonoff is a Ukrainian journalist who has already lost one country and one future. Her mind is breaking under what she has seen, and it holds a secret that could decide the fate of millions. She never meant to lead anyone. But on the flooded margins of Warsaw she finds a scattered community of survivors and begins to do the one thing she knows how to do: ask them what happened, and write it down. A girl of nineteen hoards photographs of the life that was taken from her. A fourteen-year-old cannot stop stealing. A Mariupol veteran that cannot stop hearing. A six-year-old who has not spoken since the invasion notices everything. Together they form what Diana calls the Protocols, a record of testimonies, diaries and field notes, against the day someone needs to know the truth.

When the occupation ends, Diana has vanished. She leaves behind her papers and a note: "Tell them what we did." Alex sets out across a broken country to find the people she wrote about and hear the rest in their own voices.

Sixteen testimonies. Sixteen people, each wrestling with a question of conscience as old as the Ten Commandments. *The Syren Protocols* is a novel of an occupied city told by the people who stayed in it: urgent, human, and stubborn about hope.

*For readers of* World War Z, On the Beach *and Kieślowski's* Dekalog.

## Short version

For catalogues, social posts and the top of a query letter.

Warsaw has fallen and flooded, and a living weapon is spreading through the water, pacifying everyone it touches. Diana Antonoff, a Ukrainian journalist, spends the occupation collecting the testimonies of the people who refuse to be calm. When she vanishes, Alex follows her trail. As waters rise and liberties fall, who can be trusted when the mind itself becomes a battlefield? A multi-voice novel in sixteen Protocols, built from diaries, recordings and field notes.
